Problem space

The problem is not simply drainage. It is reliable, patient-appropriate drainage over time.

Hydrocephalus shunts save lives, but obstruction, infection, mechanical failure, and improper drainage still drive repeat procedures. Published research estimates roughly 40% fail within the first year, with $1.4–$2.0 billion in annual shunt-related hospital costs.

01

A high-cost reliability problem

Obstruction, infection, mechanical problems, and drainage mismatch can still end in revision surgery.

02

One valve does not see one physiology

CSF and venous pressures vary across patients and over time. A fixed valve must operate across that changing environment.

03

Endovascular shunting opens a new design space

Endovascular CSF diversion creates an opportunity for smaller shunts with better control, monitoring, and long-term reliability.

Our approach

Our wedge: make the valve more adaptive, observable, and failure-resistant.

AgiNeuro focuses on the valve: regulating drainage across changing pressure conditions while making shunt performance easier to verify.

  • Design around obstruction, mechanical failure, overdrainage, and underdrainage.
  • Test flow regulation and post-implant adjustability.
  • Use sensing to identify obstruction and abnormal drainage.
  • Validate through clinician feedback, bench testing, and preclinical development.

Technology direction

A next-generation shunt is a fluid-control system, not just a tube.

The goal is not one fixed flow rate. It is safe, controlled drainage across changing CSF and venous pressures.

Adaptive CSF valve architecture

  • Design around the CSF-to-venous pressure gradient.
  • Control the pressure-flow curve to limit under- and overdrainage.
  • Compare fixed, flow-regulated, and adjustable valves.

Shunt intelligence layer

  • Sense pressure, flow, and device performance.
  • Detect changes in shunt performance over time.
  • Give clinicians actionable device data.
